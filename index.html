Abdullah al-Ahsan

Historian of civilizations

Human dignity and the fate of civilizations.

Across more than three decades of teaching and research, Abdullah al-Ahsan has examined how societies cohere, how institutions carry moral ideas, how civilizations encounter one another, and what happens when dignity gives way to domination.

Civilizations are born and progress when they recognize and practice human dignity, and they decline and fall when they stop.

Questions that remain current

Three questions open the body of work.

Across his books, articles, teaching and public scholarship, three recurring problems connect political order, civilizational encounter and the writing of history.

01

What makes political order worthy of human beings?

His work on dignity, law and governance asks how moral recognition becomes institutional practice — and what follows when institutions lose that purpose.

Qur'anic Guidance for Good Governance · “Law, Religion and Human Dignity in the Muslim World Today”

Explore →

02

Must civilizations meet through dialogue — or through clash?

A sustained strand of the scholarship examines the intellectual costs of “clash” paradigms and the historical possibilities of cooperation across civilizational difference.

Dialogue of Civilizations: Islamic and Western Perceptions · writings on Huntington and Bernard Lewis

Read →

03

Who gets to define the historical record?

From Eurocentrism to the modern nation-state, his work asks how the historian’s vantage point shapes what becomes visible, whose agency is recognized, and how the present reads the past.

Ummah or Nation · “Problems of Eurocentric Views of History”
