Civilizational Unity · Historian of Civilizations

Abdullah al‑Ahsan

Civilizations are born and progress when they recognize and practice human dignity, and they decline and fall when they stop.

Five books and more than fifty publications. More than thirty years of academic teaching and research, including appointments at International Islamic University Malaysia and Istanbul Şehir University. PhD in History, University of Michigan. Fellow of the Caux Round Table.

The Dignity Framework · four links

A source-linked reading of Abdullah al-Ahsan’s published argument: recognition of human dignity supports cooperation; durable cooperation requires governance; and these conditions shape civilizational progress. Use the control to examine the same relationship in reverse as dignity erodes.

Stephen B. Young, Global Executive Director, Caux Round Table
Published on cauxroundtable.org, March 2020

The Caux Round Table was founded in 1986 by Frits Philips, former president of Philips Electronics, and Olivier Giscard d'Estaing of INSEAD, with Ryuzaburo Kaku of Canon pressing the case for corporate responsibility. It was formed to reduce escalating trade tensions through a principled approach to global business, and its Principles for Business remain a reference standard in business ethics.

  • A fourteen-year working partnership across two intellectual traditions, which is the dialogue thesis practiced rather than argued

What he works on

Every theme is anchored in published work and in courses he has taught.

Rise and Fall of Civilizations6 works

What makes a civilization cohere, and what the record shows about how cohesion is lost.

Dialogue versus Clash9 works

A sustained, documented rebuttal of Huntington and Bernard Lewis, and the case for an alternative paradigm.

Human Dignity, Law and Governance6 works

Including two co-edited volumes with Stephen B. Young and a study of the OIC's Cairo Declaration.

Islam and the West, Orientalism5 works, 4 courses

How each tradition has read the other, and what the misreadings have cost.

The OIC and Muslim World Institutions9 works

A four-decade institutional history, from the 1988 monograph to the OIC at fifty.

Ummah, Nation and the Modern State7 works

The identity question Ummah or Nation opened in 1992 and that has not closed since.

Eurocentrism and the Method of History7 works

How the discipline's default vantage point shapes what counts as evidence, and what to do about it.

Contemporary Crises in Historical Perspective14 works
