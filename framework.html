A reading guide to the work

Governance, dignity, cooperation, flourishing.

Civilizations are born and progress when they recognize and practice human dignity, and they decline and fall when they stop.

Abdullah al-Ahsan supplied that sentence for this site. The four categories below group the published work behind it — governance, dignity, cooperation and flourishing — so a reader can go from the claim to the article or book where it is made.

The four groupings are a curatorial index to publications listed on the Writing page. They are not a named method, and they are not drawn from the wording of any single article.

The second half of the sentence

Decline is argued as directly as flourishing

“…and they decline and fall when they stop.” The clause is not a rhetorical flourish; several publications take it as their subject, most recently on Palestine and Gaza.

  • Israel’s Gaza War: A Symbol of Collapsing Civilization, in Genocide in Gaza: Voices of Global Conscience, eds. Ahmet Davutoglu and Richard Falk (Clarity Press, 2025), 121–136.

Historical lenses

Where to test the categories

Four places in the published record where the categories stop being abstract.

Early Muslim polity

Oceanic trade, 15th–16th centuries

Muslim and European traders used the same word for what they were doing and meant different things by it. The article follows that gap into the politics that came after.

Muslim and European Perceptions of Oceanic ‘Trade’ in the Fifteenth and Sixteenth Centuries and their Implications in International Politics, Intellectual Discourse 7:2 (1999), 172–188

For a seminar or reading group

Questions worth keeping open

  1. Whose dignity is recognized in the case under study — and whose is not?
  2. Which institutions carry moral commitments into practice, and which only claim to?
  3. What makes cooperation possible across difference?
  4. Where does coercion substitute for cooperation, and at what cost?
  5. What evidence in the case cuts against the reading offered here?
  6. Would the same interpretation survive comparison with another period or civilization?

These questions are study prompts prepared by Civilizational Unity for use alongside the cited work. They are not quotations from Professor al-Ahsan.
