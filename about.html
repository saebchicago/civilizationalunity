The scholar and the project

A lifetime of inquiry into civilizations, institutions and human dignity.

Civilizational Unity is a public intellectual home for the scholarship of Abdullah al-Ahsan. It is organized to help readers encounter the questions, books, articles and lectures themselves—and to see the continuity among them.

Abdullah al-Ahsan

Historian of civilizations

Abdullah al-Ahsan holds a PhD in History from the University of Michigan, an MA in Islamic Studies from McGill University, and an MS in History from Quaid-i-Azam University. He taught at the International Islamic University Malaysia from 1990 to 2017 and later served as Professor of Comparative Civilization at Istanbul Şehir University.

Across five books and more than fifty publications listed in the curriculum vitae he supplied for this site, his work returns to questions of civilizational identity, human dignity, governance, Muslim-world institutions, dialogue and conflict, and the assumptions through which history itself is written.

He has served as Professor and Deputy Dean at ISTAC, Director of IIUM Press, editor of Intellectual Discourse, a visiting professor at Virginia Theological Seminary, and is a Fellow of the Caux Round Table for Moral Capitalism.

The intellectual arc

Questions that recur across the record

The subjects change across decades; several underlying questions do not.

Civilizational identity

How do communities understand belonging when the moral vocabulary of the ummah, the modern nation-state and inherited historical identities pull in different directions?

Dialogue and conflict

What does the historical record show about Muslim–Western encounter, and what is obscured when civilizational conflict is treated as inevitable?

Institutions and governance

How are moral commitments translated—or not translated—into durable political institutions, from the OIC to broader questions of good governance?

Historical vantage point

How does the historian’s point of view shape what counts as evidence, agency and civilizational significance?

Why Civilizational Unity exists

An intellectual gateway, not a monument

The purpose of this site is practical: make a dispersed scholarly record easier to find, read, teach, cite and discuss. The homepage offers a short route into the ideas; the Writing page carries the broader bibliography; the Ideas page traces a recurring argument; the Talks page opens the work to seminars and public conversation.

The project is intentionally restrained about attribution. Direct quotations are identified as such. Bibliographic and biographical details are tied to the supplied CV or external records. Reading guides and thematic groupings are curatorial aids, not substitutes for the publications themselves.

Access and continuity

Built to remain readable

The site uses a lightweight static architecture, semantic HTML, keyboard-visible focus, a skip link, responsive layouts, reduced-motion support and no autoplaying media. WCAG 2.2 AA is the design target rather than a claim of formal certification.

Where videos are hosted by third parties, caption and transcript quality remains dependent on the source channel unless a separately verified transcript is provided. Corrections, source questions and accessibility barriers can be sent through the contact form.
