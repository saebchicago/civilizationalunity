About Abdullah al-Ahsan

Abdullah al-Ahsan

Historian of civilizations

Abdullah al-Ahsan holds a PhD in History from the University of Michigan, an MA in Islamic Studies from McGill University, and an MS in History from Quaid-i-Azam University. He taught at the International Islamic University Malaysia from 1990 to 2017 and later served as Professor of Comparative Civilization at Istanbul Şehir University.

Across five books and more than fifty publications listed in his CV, his work returns to questions of civilizational identity, human dignity, governance, Muslim-world institutions, dialogue and conflict, and the assumptions through which history itself is written.

He has served as Professor and Deputy Dean at ISTAC, Director of IIUM Press, editor of Intellectual Discourse, a visiting professor at Virginia Theological Seminary, and is a Fellow of the Caux Round Table for Moral Capitalism.

The intellectual arc

Four questions across the work

The subjects change across decades; several underlying questions do not.

Civilizational identity

How do communities understand belonging when the moral vocabulary of the ummah, the modern nation-state and inherited historical identities pull in different directions?

Dialogue and conflict

What does the historical record show about Muslim–Western encounter, and what is obscured when civilizational conflict is treated as inevitable?

Institutions and governance

How are moral commitments translated into durable political institutions, or left untranslated, from the OIC to broader questions of good governance?

Historical vantage point

How does the historian’s point of view shape what counts as evidence, agency and civilizational significance?
