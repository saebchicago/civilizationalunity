Five talks

Topics

  • Dialogue or Clash: What the Record Actually Shows

    A direct response to Huntington and Bernard Lewis, built from the history of Muslim and European contact rather than from prediction. Audiences leave with a testable alternative framework instead of a slogan. Draws on nine published works.

  • Rise and Fall: How Civilizations Come Apart

    The four-link argument in full, run forward and then inverted. Suited to a general or undergraduate audience, and to leadership groups thinking about institutional decline.

  • Human Dignity Before Law: Reading the Cairo Declaration

    What happens when a human rights instrument is written by states rather than for persons. Based on his study in the Journal of Law and Religion and his two volumes with Stephen B. Young.

  • The OIC at Fifty and After

    A four-decade institutional history from someone who has tracked the organization since 1988, covering conflict resolution, the Rohingya crisis and the limits of collective Muslim diplomacy.

  • Eurocentrism and the Method of History

    For history departments and graduate seminars: how the discipline's default vantage point determines what counts as evidence, and what a working alternative method looks like.
